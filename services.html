The Process

How the solution works

Glaubark works directly with farmers and institutional partners to design and implement carbon farming projects — from the first site visit through to carbon credit issuance and market access.

1

Identifying farmers and assessing land

01

Glaubark identifies suitable project areas and farmer groups, working with FPOs, sugar factories, and local partners to select locations where sustainable farming can create strong environmental and livelihood impact.

Site selection Stakeholder mapping Eligibility assessment
2

Baseline surveys, data collection, and farmer onboarding

02

Farmers are onboarded through a structured process including registration, consent, land and crop details, and baseline farming data. Digital multilingual surveys, geotagging, GIS mapping, and audio/video documentation ensure transparency and audit readiness.

Digital surveys GIS mapping Multilingual tools Farmer consent
3

Supporting adoption of sustainable farming practices

03

Glaubark supports farmers in implementing locally suitable regenerative practices — including crop residue management, organic inputs, drip irrigation, crop rotation, reduced tillage, intercropping, biodiversity enhancement, and natural pest management systems.

Practice training Field demonstrations Capacity building
4

Monitoring through technology and field data

04

The company monitors project progress through field data collection, satellite imagery, remote sensing, GIS mapping, digital surveys, and MRV frameworks — ensuring outcomes are measurable, transparent, and verifiable.

Satellite imagery Remote sensing MRV dashboards Carbon accounting
5

Validation, verification, and carbon credit issuance

05

Glaubark prepares project documentation, coordinates third-party validation and verification, and supports the issuance of verified carbon credits through recognised standards.

Third-party verification Registry support VCS compliance
6

Voluntary carbon market access

06

After credits are issued, Glaubark connects project partners with the voluntary carbon market — supporting the positioning, sale, or retirement of credits in line with their sustainability and climate goals.

Market access Credit retirement Climate finance

Full Project Cycle

11 stages of the carbon project cycle

Every Glaubark carbon project moves through a structured, end-to-end process — from the first site assessment to carbon credit issuance and market access. Scroll to walk through every stage.

Stage 01 / 11

Stage 01

Project identification and site selection

Glaubark identifies suitable project geographies based on farmer need, land suitability, crop type, climate conditions, soil quality, and carbon potential — working with FPOs, sugar factories, NGOs, and district-level partners.

Site selection Stakeholder mapping Eligibility assessment
1

Stage 02

Farmer outreach and stakeholder consultation

The project begins with trust building. Glaubark conducts consultations with farmers, village councils, FPOs, and local partners to explain the model, carbon credit potential, farmer responsibilities, and long-term outcomes.

Community engagement FPO partnerships Trust building
2

Stage 03

Farmer onboarding and data collection

Farmers are onboarded through a structured process including registration, consent, land and crop details, and baseline farming data. Digital multilingual surveys, geotagging, GIS mapping, and audio/video documentation ensure transparency and audit readiness.

Digital surveys GIS mapping Multilingual tools Farmer consent
3

Stage 04

Baseline assessment

Establishes the starting conditions of the farm — assessing practices, crop patterns, soil conditions, irrigation methods, fertilizer use, and emission-relevant activities. A strong baseline is essential for additionality and carbon credit integrity.

Soil analysis Emission baseline Additionality check
4

Stage 05

Project design and methodology selection

Glaubark designs the project execution plan, selects sustainable farming interventions, identifies the appropriate carbon methodology, and creates the implementation roadmap aligned with recognised carbon standards.

VCS methodology Project design document Intervention roadmap
5

Stage 06

Sustainable farming practice implementation

Farmers are supported in adopting locally suitable regenerative practices that improve soil health, reduce input dependency, increase carbon sequestration, and build long-term agricultural productivity.

Regenerative practices Input reduction Field monitoring
6

Stage 07

Farmer training and capacity building

Glaubark provides awareness sessions, field demonstration shows, practice guidance, and ongoing advisory to ground teams and farmers to ensure correct and consistent implementation across all enrolled plots.

Practice training Field demonstrations Capacity building
7

Stage 08

Monitoring, reporting, and verification support

Glaubark builds the MRV framework to track project progress — collecting field evidence, coordinating satellite and remote sensing data, and preparing monitoring reports that are transparent, auditable, and verifiable.

Satellite imagery MRV dashboards Carbon accounting
