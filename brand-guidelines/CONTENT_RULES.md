# Glaubark Content Rules

Concise verbal identity for anyone writing website copy, LinkedIn, decks, case studies, emails, or reports.

Facts in this file are limited to what the client brief and proofing log support. **Do not copy statistics from the current homepage into new work.**

Classification tags: **Observed** · **Brand Rule** · **Strategic Recommendation** · **Client Confirmation Required**

Master narrative: `BRAND_GUIDELINES.md`. Unverified inventory: `CLIENT-REVIEW-ASSUMED-CONTENT.md`.

## 1. Voice

Glaubark sounds like a field-literate climate-tech company: specific, calm, commercially serious, respectful of farmers.

| We are | We are not |
|---|---|
| Clear | Mystical |
| Specific | Vague (“impact at scale”) |
| Evidence-led | Inspirational poster copy |
| Grounded in Indian agriculture | Generic global NGO |
| Practical | Consultancy-speak |
| Human | SaaS onboarding copy |
| Technical when it earns trust | Crypto / “on-chain credits” |

**Brand Rule:** Write as if a project developer, an agronomist, and a farmer could all finish the paragraph. If only a marketer could, rewrite.

## 3. Sentence style (Observed → Brand Rule)

- Prefer short to medium sentences. One idea each.
- Headlines in **sentence case**.
- Eyebrows: **uppercase**, tracked, 11px equivalent in product UI.
- CTAs: verb + object, sentence case — “Get in touch”, “Subscribe”, “Explore services”.
- Avoid exclamation marks.
- Avoid “we’re passionate about” / “reimagining the future of”.
- Oxford comma optional; be consistent within a piece.
- Use Indian English spelling in long copy when writing for India (**organisation**, **fertiliser** already appears). Do not mix “fertilizer” and “fertiliser” in one document.

## 4. How to talk about…

### Farmers

**Do say:** farmers, farming families, enrolled farmers, FPOs, field teams, districts, talukas, crops by name (sugarcane, etc. when true).

**Avoid:** beneficiaries, the poor, “smallholders” as a pity frame, “empowering communities” with no mechanism, smiling-stock captions, invented names.

Farmers are **partners in a project**, not recipients of charity.

### Climate

**Do say:** climate action, climate finance, climate resilience, soil carbon, emissions reductions, regenerative land management — tied to practices.

**Avoid:** saving the planet, net-zero theatre, melting ice, polar bears, wind-turbine-as-climate.

### Carbon

**Do say:** carbon projects, carbon credits, voluntary carbon markets, MRV, validation, verification, recognised standards, **VCS project support** (wording from the brief).

**Avoid until confirmed:** “VCS certified”, “registry-listed”, “third-party verified” as a present-tense company badge, “high-integrity credits already issued”, tokenised / Web3 carbon.

If status is “in design / in development”, say that.

### Technology

**Do say:** satellite, remote sensing, GIS, digital / multilingual surveys, field measurement, MRV systems — **tools used in the field**.

**Avoid:** “agentic AI”, “our platform”, “dashboard-first”, generic SaaS screenshots that do not exist, crypto rails.

Glaubark is not an accounting-SaaS or an AI product. Partners may include precision-ag / AI farming companies; that is partnership language, not product language.

### Impact

**Do say:** soil health, water efficiency, lower input costs (qualitative unless a number is approved), carbon sequestration as an outcome of practices, market access, long-term field relationships.

**Avoid:** any percentage, rupee figure, hectare total, farmer headcount, or “average uplift” that is not in an approved fact sheet.

The homepage numbers **2,800%**, **88.8%**, **−52%**, **−93%**, **−80%**, **×5** are **invented or template leftovers**. Never reuse them.

## 5. Messaging that is safe to reuse (from brief — still confirm if stale)

These are **positioning**, not performance claims:

- Farmer-first carbon project developer.
- Work sits at agriculture + climate finance + regenerative farming + rural development.
- Maharashtra districts named in the brief: Pune, Sangli, Satara, Kolhapur, Solapur (and other agricultural districts).
- Partner *types*: sugar factories, FPOs, AI / precision-ag companies.
- Partner *geographies* named: Meghalaya, Punjab, Kerala — not a claim of offices in each.
- Practices as a **menu**: residue management, drip, organic inputs, rotation, reduced tillage, intercropping, and others listed on Services — not “every farm gets 10+ interventions” unless confirmed.
- Cycle: identification → onboarding → baseline → design → practices → training → MRV → validation/verification **support** → voluntary market access.

Vision (on About, nearly verbatim from brief):

> To make Indian agriculture a global model for climate action, farmer prosperity, and regenerative land management.

Mission (slightly shortened on site — **Client Confirmation Required** if you need the brief’s exact sentence):

> To empower farmers through regenerative practices, generate high-integrity carbon credits, and unlock climate finance as a new source of rural income.

Goal: brief said “one of India’s leading farmer-centric carbon developers.” Site says “foremost.” Prefer the brief until the client chooses otherwise.

Hectares: brief “close to covering 100,000 hectares” — **Client Confirmation Required** before repeating as current fact. Do **not** invent a farmer count; the brief left it blank.

## 8. Claims protocol (Brand Rule)

Before publishing a number, name, logo, or certification:

1. Is it in the client brief or a signed fact sheet?
2. If not, is it in `CLIENT-REVIEW-ASSUMED-CONTENT.md` as invented/assumed? → **do not use**.
3. Named person: written permission, real photo, real location.
4. Standard / registry: current status in one clause (“support”, “in development”, “registered”, “issued”).
5. When unsure, omit. Empty space is more on-brand than a fake stat.

**Never**

- Invented testimonials (Rajesh Kumar, Priya Nair, Arvind Mehta on `index2`/`index3`; Ramesh Patil; Suresh Mane)
- Pravatar / Unsplash faces as staff or farmers
- “Glaubark Editorial” as a fake byline presenting fiction as reportage
- Partner logos not supplied by the partner
- Exact emails, reply-time promises, minimum acreage, or co-branding offers unless confirmed

Contact details on the live Contact page (`hello@…`, Instagram `@glaubark`, 24h reply, 50–100 farmers / 500–1,000 acres, 4–8 week design) are **assumed**. Treat as **Client Confirmation Required**.

## 10. Audience cues (Strategic Recommendation)

Same facts; different lead.

| Audience | Lead with | Do not lead with |
|---|---|---|
| Farmers / FPOs | Practices, soil, water, costs, how enrolment works, who visits the farm | Registry jargon, buyer logos |
| Carbon project partners | Lifecycle, MRV, standards alignment, districts, partner types | Charity framing |
| Carbon buyers | Integrity of process, traceability to farms, what is / is not issued | Inflated vintage volumes |
| Climate organisations | Farmer participation + measurement | “Tech disruption” |
| Finance / institutions | Governance of the cycle, documentation, market access | Lifestyle photography only |
| Tech / implementation partners | Field workflow, data capture, interoperability | Fake dashboards |

## 11. CTA philosophy

CTAs on the site today mix solid process (“Contact”, “Subscribe”) with overclaiming (“transform your farmland into a carbon asset”).

**Brand Rule for new work**

- One primary CTA per view.
- Promise a conversation or a document, not a guaranteed credit payment.
- Farmer CTAs: enrolment / talk to the field team.
- Buyer / partner CTAs: discuss a project / request a briefing.
