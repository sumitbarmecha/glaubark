01

Who Is Glaubark? — The Photographer's Context

Brand in one paragraph

Glaubark Solutions is an Indian agri-carbon company that helps smallholder farmers — primarily sugarcane growers in Maharashtra and Karnataka — adopt regenerative farming practices. These practices restore soil health, reduce input costs, and sequester carbon. The resulting CO₂ reductions are measured via satellite + field monitoring, verified by independent auditors, and issued as VCS-aligned carbon credits sold to global corporate buyers. Farmers earn a new income stream from the carbon their improved soil locks away. Glaubark bridges two worlds: rural Indian agriculture and global climate finance.

Every image or video you create for Glaubark must communicate one of two things: the dignity and transformation of Indian farming communities, or the science and rigour behind the carbon outcomes they generate. The visual language sits between documentary journalism and premium editorial — never stock, never staged, never pity.

02

Visual Philosophy

🌱

Grounded & Honest

Real fields, real farmers, real soil. No overproduced setups. The camera should feel like it belongs there. Documentary instinct with editorial polish.

🔬

Scientific & Precise

Technology and monitoring are part of the brand. Shots of tablets, soil probes, data collection, and satellite-referenced maps are as important as the field images.

☀️

Hopeful & Forward

This is not poverty porn. Farmers are skilled professionals transforming their land. The emotional register is confidence, optimism, and ownership — not hardship.

🇮🇳

Indian, Specific, Local

Sugarcane fields, red soil, Maharashtra light, Kolhapur saris, Karnataka hills. Avoid generic "global agriculture" aesthetics. This story is rooted here.

🎨

On-Brand Palette

Deep greens, golden hour amber, earthy ochres, lime-tinted highlights. The visual palette should feel like it belongs to the brand without being forced.

📐

Intentional Composition

Leave breathing room. Think about where text will sit. Horizontal thirds, negative space in sky, rows of crops as natural leading lines.

03

Lighting & Time of Day

💡

Golden hour is your primary working window. Schedule all farmer portraits, field detail shots, and drone flights for 30 minutes after sunrise and 45 minutes before sunset. The Indian agricultural landscape is most photogenic and brand-aligned in warm, raking light.

Time Quality Best For Avoid
5:30 – 7:00 AM Soft, blue-to-amber transition. Morning mist if lucky. Farmer arrival shots, early irrigation, mist over fields, drone altitude sweeps Portrait close-ups (too cool)
7:00 – 9:00 AM Golden, directional, warm. Best of the day. All farmer portraits, field activity, soil close-ups, drone patterns Nothing — this is the priority window
9:00 – 10:30 AM Bright, slightly harder. Still good for technology shots. Tablet/app use, group training, equipment shots Skin-tone sensitive portraits
10:30 AM – 4:00 PM Harsh, flat overhead. Kills depth and warmth. Controlled indoor shots, equipment detail, document photography Any field or farmer photography
4:00 – 6:00 PM Returning warmth. Slightly softer than morning. Field activity, community shots, group training sessions Drone (often too hazy)
6:00 – 6:45 PM Amber, cinematic, backlit magic. Hero editorial shots, silhouettes, wide field scenes, final drone run Technical documentation shots
04

Shot Categories — Directions for Each

Each category below maps to a specific section or need on the Glaubark website. Deliver a minimum of 15 selects per category unless noted.

A Fields & Landscape Min. 20 selects
  • 1

    The Open Field — Hero Width

    Full-width landscape, ideally with the entire field visible from one end, natural rows of sugarcane or crop running as leading lines toward a horizon. Sky in the upper third. This is the hero banner image for every page — must work with text overlaid on it.

    Ultra-wide / 14–24mm Golden hour only Expansive, quiet, hopeful
  • 2

    Crop Row — Walk-Through Perspective

    Camera low (knee height), looking down the tunnel formed by tall sugarcane or maize rows. Blurred leading lines converging to a point of light at the end. A farmer may be partially visible in the far distance — optional.

    35mm, low angle, f/2.8–4 Backlit morning Immersive, scale, depth
  • 3

    Soil Close-Up — Hand in Earth

    Farmer's hand scooping or holding dark, rich soil. Focus on texture — visible aggregates, fine root hair, organic matter. Shoot with a macro lens or close-focus wide-angle. The hand should be weathered but dignified, not grimy.

    Macro / 100mm, extreme close Soft diffused or shade Scientific, tactile, reverent
  • 4

    Root System & Underground Shot

    Carefully excavate a 30cm section of soil wall to expose root networks. Shoot the root mass against the soil cross-section. This is powerful for carbon sequestration storytelling — roots = carbon = underground wealth. No mess, no mud smears on lens side.

    Macro, eye level with soil Reflected fill card recommended Scientific, revealing
  • 5

    Before & After Soil Contrast

    If possible, shoot conventional (lighter, depleted) soil beside regenerative (darker, richer) soil from enrolled plots. Side-by-side comparison in a single frame or matched pair. Critical for website's impact section.

    Consistent framing both shots Shade or overcast (consistent) Evidence, contrast, proof
B Farmer Portraits & Human Moments Min. 20 selects
  • 1

    Standing Portrait — In the Field

    Farmer standing upright, at ease, in or beside their crop. Confident, not posed. Eye contact with camera for authority. Use the field as background (not blurred out entirely — keep context). Shoot both vertical (for cards) and horizontal (for banners).

    85mm, f/2–2.8, eye level Golden hour, backlit OK if fill used Dignity, ownership, pride
  • 2

    Working Portrait — Mid-Action

    Farmer engaged in a specific task: adjusting drip line, applying bio-input, writing in a survey book, checking a plant stalk. Shoot candidly — do not ask them to look at camera. Two or three rapid burst sequences per activity so you have natural expressions to select from.

    35–50mm, candid distance Any directional light Purposeful, skilled, active
  • 3

    Environmental Wide — Farmer + Landscape

    Pull back to show the farmer as a small figure in a large, lush landscape. This communicates scale and relationship between person and land. Farmer in the lower third, sky and field occupying the rest. Works especially well at golden hour with silhouette.

    24–35mm, wide, low horizon Backlit golden hour Epic, connected, purposeful
  • 4

    Community & Group Moments

    2–4 farmers together, either in conversation, looking at a tablet together, or gathered at a plot boundary. Must feel natural, not staged. Candid preferred. Include FPO coordinators with farmers where possible — shows the support structure.

    35mm, slight distance Open shade or morning Collaborative, trusted, community
  • 5

    Training Session

    Glaubark field coordinator demonstrating a practice to a group of farmers. Shoot from several angles — the coordinator's perspective looking at the group, the group's perspective watching, and a wide showing both. Include the crop or soil being discussed in frame.

    Multiple: 24mm wide + 85mm tele Open shade or morning Learning, transfer, expertise
C Technology, Data & MRV Systems Min. 15 selects
  • 1

    Farmer + Tablet / Smartphone Survey

    Farmer or field coordinator using the Glaubark digital survey app in the field. Screen content should be partially visible. The field should be in background — never in an office. This communicates field-tech integration. Shoot from behind the shoulder to include both face and screen, and also a tight shot of hands on screen with crop visible behind.

    50mm over-shoulder + 85mm portrait Shade (screen readability) Modern, precise, field-native
  • 2

    Soil Sampling Equipment

    Auger, probe, or sample bags laid on soil or being used. Include the field context. Label bags visible if possible. Also shoot the sample collection sequence: probe into ground → extract → bag → label. These shots support the "science and verification" narrative.

    Close detail + mid-distance context Morning, directional Rigorous, scientific, trusted
  • 3

    GPS Mapping & Plot Boundary Walk

    Field coordinator walking the plot boundary with a GPS device or phone app. Capture both the walking action and a over-shoulder shot of the GPS screen showing the plot shape being traced. Drone shot of the plot boundary being walked is ideal if coordination is possible.

    35mm candid + drone overhead Morning, clear sky Methodical, transparent, documented
  • 4

    Drip Irrigation System

    Close-up of drip emitters delivering water to the root zone of sugarcane. Capture water droplets if possible. Also wide shot showing the full extent of drip lines running through a planted row. Farmer adjusting a dripper head — hands only, or with full figure. This is a key practice image.

    Macro for emitter + 35mm wide Backlit droplets (morning) Precision, resource efficiency
D Nature, Biodiversity & Ecosystem Detail Min. 10 selects
  • 1

    Pollinators & Beneficial Insects

    Bees, butterflies, or beneficial wasps on flowers in or near the regenerative field. Signals ecosystem health returning — biodiversity as evidence of soil and practice improvement. Patience required. Early morning for best insect activity.

    100mm macro, eye-level insect Soft morning, diffused Life returning, ecosystem health
  • 2

    Cover Crop Texture & Greenery

    Low angle through cover crops (dhaincha, cowpea) showing leaf texture, plant density, and the light filtering through. Works as both a standalone image and an abstract texture for web backgrounds. Multiple framings — portrait, landscape, extreme close.

    Low angle, backlit, f/5.6–8 Backlit morning for rim light Abundance, green, alive
  • 3

    Earthworm & Soil Biology Close-Up

    Healthy soil will have visible earthworms, mycorrhizal threads, or visible aggregation. Carefully expose a small soil section and photograph the biology present. This is hard to capture well but extremely powerful for the brand's science narrative.

    Macro, fill card for light Diffused, no harsh shadow Alive, scientific, reverent
05

Video Deliverables

Deliver all video in ProRes or H.264 4K minimum. Shoot log profile (S-Log or C-Log) where possible for colour grade flexibility. Audio: lapel mic for interviews, ambient for B-roll. Provide project files where possible.

01 — Brand Story Film (Hero Video)

A 90–120 second brand film that opens on the land, introduces a farmer by name, follows one practice adoption moment, and closes with the idea of carbon as income. Narration-free preferred — let ambient sound and on-screen text carry the story. Should work silently with subtitles (website autoplay). Cinematographic quality — this is the centrepiece of the homepage.

4K / 24fps cinematic 90–120 sec final Landscape 16:9 + 9:16 social cut Log graded to brand palette

02 — Farmer Testimonial Videos (×3)

Three individual farmer interviews, each 2–3 minutes (edit to 60–90 sec for web). Filmed on location — in the field, not in a meeting room. Farmer facing slightly off-camera (not directly into lens — aim for interview-style). Ask them: what farming was like before, what changed, and what they would tell other farmers. Film in their language (Marathi/Kannada), subtitle in English. Capture extensive B-roll of each farmer at work during the interview visit.

4K interview 60–90 sec web edit Lapel mic essential Field background mandatory Subtitle-ready

03 — Process Explainer B-Roll Package

A library of 30+ B-roll clips covering every stage of the Glaubark process: farmer registration, GIS walk, soil sampling, practice demonstration, drip installation, coordinator review with tablet, and field monitoring. Each clip 10–30 seconds. No narration — these are assembled by the web team into an interactive process section. Focus on hands, tools, and detail rather than faces (faces have separate portraits).

4K / 24–30fps 30+ clips minimum 10–30 sec each Ambient audio Organized by process stage

04 — "In Action" Website Clips (×3)

Three short 15–25 second autoplay-ready clips for the homepage video gallery section. These should be visually striking, loop-able, and work muted. Concept: (1) A field monitoring walk — coordinator moving through tall cane with tablet, (2) A drip line in action — close-up water at roots, gentle crop movement, (3) A farmer's hands working the soil — turning compost, checking a root. All three should have the brand's warm, golden, field aesthetic.

4K, loop-able 15–25 sec each Silent-viewable Landscape + portrait crops
06

Drone Shot List

🚁

Equipment recommendation: DJI Mavic 3 Pro or equivalent. Fly at 6:00–7:30 AM and 5:30–6:30 PM. Check DGCA regulations for the shoot area in advance — obtain NOC if required. Keep a ground spotter present at all times. Do not fly over people without explicit consent.

📐

Top-Down Field Pattern

Directly overhead at 40–80m. The geometric pattern of crop rows, plot boundaries, and irrigation lines from above. Shoot in both morning golden light and late afternoon for colour variation.

Altitude: 40–80m AGL Orientation: Straight down (nadir) Both stills + 4K video pan Multiple fields for variety
🌅

Golden Hour Landscape Sweep

Low-altitude (15–30m) cinematic push or pull over the field at sunrise or sunset. The light should rake across the crop rows creating deep shadows and gold highlights on the leaves.

Altitude: 15–30m AGL Speed: Slow (2–4 m/s) Direction: Into light or cross-light 4K video + photo bursts
🏘️

Village-to-Field Context Shot

Pull back wide to show the farm in the context of the village — buildings, roads, surrounding landscape. Establishes that this is real India, not an isolated lab. Shows the scale of the land relative to habitation.

Altitude: 100–150m AGL Wide establishing, slow orbit Both video + still Morning light preferred
👤

Farmer Walking the Plot — Overhead

Coordinate with the farmer to walk slowly along a crop row while drone follows from 20–25m directly above. The figure moving through the crop, seen from above, is a powerful visual metaphor — a person in their element, surrounded by growth.

Altitude: 20–25m AGL Nadir or slight angle (30°) Farmer walks slowly, no looking up 4K video essential
🔄

Orbit Around Farmer / Landmark Tree

360° orbit around a farmer standing in the field or a prominent tree on the farm boundary. Smooth, slow full revolution at eye level or slightly above. Creates a cinematic hero moment — strong for the brand story film.

Altitude: 3–6m AGL Radius: 8–12m from subject Full 360° in 20–30 sec 4K video, stabilised
📊

Conventional vs. Regenerative Comparison

If access permits, fly over both a conventional (neighbouring) farm and the enrolled regenerative plot in the same flight. Side-by-side top-down shots that visually compare crop density, colour saturation, and field health. Extremely powerful for website impact sections.

Matched framing both shots Same altitude, same time of day Still photography + video pan Coordinate access in advance

Do's & Don'ts — Non-Negotiable

Always Do

  • Shoot in RAW format — no exceptions
  • Obtain verbal or written consent from every farmer photographed
  • Keep the field in frame — context is everything
  • Prioritise golden hour for all field work
  • Shoot both horizontal (16:9) and vertical (4:5, 9:16) variants of key images
  • Leave sky/negative space room for text overlay in banner images
  • Include close-ups AND establishing wides for every scene
  • Label all deliverables clearly by category and location
  • Capture ambient audio with every video sequence
  • Shoot with the brand palette in mind (greens, golds, earthy tones)
  • Respect the farmer's dignity in every frame — no pity aesthetics
  • Check every frame for distracting elements (litter, unrelated signage)

Never Do

  • Shoot in harsh midday light for any field or portrait work
  • Photograph farmers without consent or understanding
  • Use heavy filtering or over-saturated presets — editorial restraint
  • Stage scenes that don't reflect actual farming activity
  • Include branded materials from other companies in frame
  • Frame farmers in ways that emphasise poverty or hardship
  • Photograph only faces — always include hands, tools, land context
  • Deliver JPEGs only — RAW files mandatory alongside edits
  • Fly the drone without proper clearances in restricted zones
  • Use wide-angle distortion on portrait close-ups (minimum 50mm for faces)
  • Submit blurry or soft images — every select must be technically sharp
  • Crop tightly on sensitive cultural or personal elements without consent

Grading direction: Lift the shadows slightly in greens. Keep skin tones warm and accurate — do not push Indian skin tones toward orange or desaturate toward grey. In general, slight warmth in highlights, cooler midtones, lifted and slightly green-tinged shadows. Think: Indian golden hour, not Californian desert or Scandinavian cool.
